Even with energy labels, comparing running costs between TV models is still difficult with all of the different companies, sizes, technologies and features. Without clear comparisons, this can lead to higher electricity consumtion and bigger bills. The information is out there, but most shoppers never see it.

What You Can Do

  • Check the star rating and annual kWh on the energy label before buying.
  • Use Eco mode, lower the brightness, and turn off always listening voice features to reduce standby draw.
  • Size matters more then the brand. A well-chosen 55 inch LED TV will usually cot less to run than a poorly rated 65 inch model.

What the Data Says

These findings come from the Australian Government's Television Energy Rating dataset. They show what is actually on the Autralian market.

Chart 1: What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

LED-based TVs are the default choice for most Australian household and OLED is a premium niche.

Chart 2: What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

55-inch and 65-inch TVs are the most frequent sizes on the market, with 65-inch models slightly ahead. 75-inch TVs are also common. Smaller sizes under 40 inches are becoming rare as prices for larger panels continue to drop. This confirms that Australians are buying bigger screens than ever before.

Chart 3:Which brands have the largest number of models?

Kogan, LG and Samsung Electronics have the widest range of TV models available in Australis.

Chart 4: Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

LCD TVs consume the least power (median 70W), followed by LCD (LED) at 105W and OLED at 113W. OLEDs use slightly more power but offer better contrast and viewing angles. The gap is not huge, but it adds up over years of daily use.

Chart 5: What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

There is a clear positive relationship between screen size and power use. A 55-inch TV typically draws around 100–200W, while a 75-inch model can draw 200–400W, and the largest 100-inch+ TVs can exceed 500W. Size is the single biggest factor in how much power a TV uses.

Chart 6: What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

Star ratings are evenly distributed across different screen sizes. You don't have to sacrifice efficieny for size as a high star ratings do appear across all sizes. A large TV can still be energy-smart if you choose the right model.

Chart 7: Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

This chart only includes brands with the highest count in the dataset. JVC and Sylvox generally have lower median power consumption, while Samsung, Sony, and Samsung Electronics tend to have higher median consumption.

Chart 8: Which TV brands have the highest and lowest average power consumption?

Spark Electronics has the highest average power consumption at 233.45W, while Tavice has the lowest at 130W
